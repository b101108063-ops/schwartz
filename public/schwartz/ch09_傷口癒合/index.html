CHAPTER 9 WOUND HEALING 傷口癒合

CHAPTER 9 WOUND HEALING 傷口癒合

來源： Schwartz’s Principles of Surgery, 11th Edition

Phases of Wound Healing 傷口癒合階段

Timeline

Phase Duration Key Events
Hemostatic/Inflammatory 0-4 days Platelet plug, fibrin clot, neutrophil infiltration, macrophage transition
Proliferative Days 4-12 Fibroblast infiltration, collagen synthesis, angiogenesis, granulation tissue
Maturation/Remodeling Weeks to months Collagen cross-linking, tensile strength increase, scar maturation

Cellular Players

Cell Timing Key Functions
Platelets Immediate Hemostasis, PDGF release
Neutrophils 0-2 days Debridement, bacteria killing, inflammation
Macrophages Days 1-2 onward Debridement, growth factor release, fibroblast recruitment
T lymphocytes ~Day 7 (peak) Bridge inflammatory→proliferative phase
Fibroblasts Days 3-5 onward Collagen synthesis, wound contraction
Endothelial cells Days 4+ Angiogenesis (VEGF, FGF, TNF-α, TGF-β)
Myofibroblasts Day 6+ Wound contraction (α-smooth muscle actin)

Hemostasis & Inflammation 止血與發炎

Hemostatic Phase

  1. Platelet adhesion to exposed collagen (vWF-mediated)。
  2. Platelet aggregation (fibrinogen receptor - GPIIb/IIIa)。
  3. Platelet activation → release of PDGF, TGF-β, EGF, fibrinogen。
  4. Fibrin clot formation (intrinsic + extrinsic pathways)。

Inflammatory Phase

  • Neutrophils first responders (0-2 days)。
  • Macrophages appear day 1-2, become dominant by day 2。
  • Macrophage activities：
    • Phagocytosis (bacteria, debris)。
    • Debridement (collagenase, elastase)。
    • Growth factor release (PDGF, TGF-β, TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6)。
    • Nitric oxide production.

Proliferative Phase 增生階段

Granulation Tissue Formation

  • New capillary formation (angiogenesis)。
  • Fibroblast proliferation and migration。
  • Collagen synthesis (types I and III)。
  • Proteoglycan/glycosaminoglycan deposition。

Angiogenesis

  • Stimulators： VEGF, FGF, TNF-α, TGF-β。
  • Endothelial cells migrate from venules near wound。
  • Form new capillary loops。

Collagen Synthesis (Biochemistry)

Key steps：

  1. mRNA transcription → protocollagen。
  2. Hydroxylation of proline/lysine (requires O₂, Fe, vitamin C, α-ketoglutarate)。
  3. Glycosylation (galactose + glucose linking)。
  4. Triple helix formation → procollagen。
  5. Secretion → extracellular cleavage → collagen monomers。
  6. Cross-linking (lysyl oxidase) → mature collagen fibrils。

Proteoglycans

  • Glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) + proteins → proteoglycans。
  • Major wound GAGs： Dermatan sulfate, chondroitin sulfate。
  • Hyaluronic acid (up to 2000 disaccharide units)。
  • Guide collagen fibril assembly。

Epithelialization 上皮化

Process

  1. Day 1： Epidermal thickened at wound edge。
  2. Basal cells lose attachment to dermis, flatten, migrate。
  3. Migration across provisional matrix (fibronectin-rich)。
  4. Mitosis in cells behind leading edge (“leapfrog” migration)。
  5. Re-establishment of layering and keratinization。

Timing

  • Approximated incised wounds：<48 hours。
  • Large wounds：Days to weeks。

Growth Factors Promoting Epithelialization

  • EGF, TGF-β, FGF, PDGF, IGF-1。

Wound Contraction 傷口攣縮

Myofibroblasts

  • α-smooth muscle actin in stress fibers。
  • Appear Day 6, peak ~Day 21。
  • Express actin by Day 6-21, then undergo apoptosis。

Mechanism

  • Fibroblast movement in collagen lattice。
  • Cytoskeletal reorganization → contraction。
  • Note： Wound contraction starts before myofibroblasts appear (role of fibroblasts)。

Growth Factors in Wound Healing 生長因子

Growth Factor Source Key Effects
PDGF Platelets, macrophages Fibroblast chemotaxis/mitogenesis, angiogenesis, collagen synthesis
FGF Fibroblasts, endothelial cells Angiogenesis, mesoderm mitogenesis
EGF Platelets, macrophages Epithelial proliferation/migration
TGF-α Keratinocytes Binds EGF receptor, mitogenic for epithelium
TGF-β Platelets, T cells, macrophages Angiogenesis, matrix production, inhibits scarring
VEGF Macrophages, fibroblasts Endothelial mitogen, angiogenesis
IGF-1 Platelets, liver Protein synthesis, glucose transport
HGF Fibroblasts Fibroblast/keratinocyte stimulation, suppresses inflammation
IL-1 Macrophages Inflammation, angiogenesis, reepithelialization
IL-6 Fibroblasts, macrophages Inflammation, angiogenesis, collagen deposition

Modes of Action

  • Autocrine： Acts on producing cell。
  • Paracrine： Acts on neighboring cells。
  • Endocrine： Acts on distant sites via bloodstream。

Scar Remodeling 疤痕重塑

Collagen Remodeling

  • MMPs (matrix metalloproteinases) break down collagen。
  • Balance：collagen synthesis ↔ collagenolysis。
  • TGF-β： ↑ collagen synthesis + ↓ MMPs (net: ↑ deposition)。

Timeline

  • Weeks 3-6： Peak collagen content reached。
  • 6-12 months： Continued cross-linking and strengthening。
  • 1-2 years： Mature, avascular, acellular scar。

Tensile Strength

Time Tensile Strength
Week 1 3-5% of original
Week 3 30%
1 year ~80% (never returns to 100%)

Scar Maturation

  • Type III collagen → Type I collagen (gradual)。
  • Fibril size increases。
  • Cross-linking increases。
  • Proteoglycan content decreases。

Heritable Connective Tissue Disorders 遺傳性結締組織疾病

Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome (EDS)

Type Defect Clinical Features
Classical (I/II) Type V collagen Hyperextensible skin, atrophic scars, hypermobile joints
Vascular (IV) Type III collagen Thin skin, visible veins, arterial/bowel/uterine rupture risk
Kyphoscoliotic (VI) Lysyl hydroxylase Hypotonia, scoliosis, joint laxity
Tenascin-X deficient TNx protein Similar to classical

Surgical Considerations in EDS

  • Close wounds in two layers。
  • Leave sutures 2× longer than usual。
  • Use external support (adhesive tape)。
  • Avoid skin traction。
  • Great care with vessel handling。

Marfan Syndrome

  • Defect： FBN1 gene (fibrillin)。
  • Features： Tall stature, arachnodactyly, lens dislocation, aortic aneurysm, hernias。
  • Note： Wound healing usually normal。

Osteogenesis Imperfecta

  • Defect： Type I collagen mutation。
  • Features： Brittle bones, blue sclera, hernias, joint laxity。
  • Surgical challenge： Bones fracture easily。

Epidermolysis Bullosa

  • Defect： Various (keratin, collagen VII, laminin, integrins)。
  • Features： Skin blistering and fragility。
  • Surgical challenge： Extremely fragile skin。

Healing in Specific Tissues 特定組織的癒合

Gastrointestinal Tract

  • Submucosa most important for anastomotic integrity。
  • Collagen synthesis in bowel wall。
  • Complications： Dehiscence, leaks, fistulas (too little healing)；strictures (too much healing)。

Bone Healing

  • Primary (direct)： Rigid fixation → Haversian remodeling。
  • Secondary (indirect)： Inflammatory callus formation。
  • Requires：stability, blood supply, adequate nutrition。

Tendon Healing

  • Extrinsic： Cell migration from sheath → adhesion formation。
  • Intrinsic： Cell migration from tendon itself。
  • Early mobilization：Improved gliding, but weaker initially。

Peripheral Nerve

  • Wallerian degeneration distal to injury。
  • Axonal regeneration：1-3 mm/day。
  • Factors affecting recovery： Age, gap length, tension。

Wound Classification 傷口分類

Type Description Healing
Primary intention Clean, edges approximated surgically Fastest, least scarring
Secondary intention Left open, heals by granulation Slower, more contraction
Tertiary (delayed primary) Initially left open, then closed surgically Combines benefits

Abnormal Wound Healing 異常傷口癒合

Hypertrophic Scar

  • Excessive collagen synthesis within wound boundaries。
  • Red, raised, pruritic。
  • Common in burn injuries。
  • Treatment： Pressure therapy, silicone gel, steroid injections。

Keloid

  • Extends beyond wound boundaries。
  • More common in dark-skinned individuals。
  • Recurs after excision。
  • Treatment： Difficult；steroid injection, radiation (limited)。

Chronic Wounds

  • Failed to progress through normal healing stages。
  • Common types： Venous stasis, diabetic foot, pressure ulcers。
  • Characteristics： Persistent inflammation, infection, hypoxia。

Wound Dehiscence

  • Partial or complete separation of wound edges。
  • Risk factors： Infection, poor nutrition, steroids, technical failure。
  • Signs： Serosanguinous drainage, “pinking” of skin。

Acute Wound Management 急性傷口處理

Surgical Wounds

  • Gentle handling of tissue。
  • Adequate hemostasis (not excessive)。
  • Minimal tension on closure。
  • Remove devitalized tissue。
  • Layered closure when appropriate。

Surgical Site Infection Prevention 手術部位感染預防

Risk Factors

  • Patient： Diabetes, obesity, smoking, malnutrition, immunosuppression。
  • Procedure： Duration, complexity, contamination。
  • Surgeon： Technique, experience。

Prophylactic Measures

  • Preoperative： Skin prep (chlorhexidine), antibiotic prophylaxis (within 60 min of incision)。
  • Intraoperative： Maintain normothermia, adequate oxygenation, minimize tissue trauma。
  • Postoperative： Wound care, monitor for infection signs。
